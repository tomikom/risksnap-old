Gamma Exposure

Gamma Exposure (GEX) = estimated change in an option dealer's hedge requirement for a 1% move in the underlying, calculated as: GEX=Γ×OI×100×S^2×0.01. Gamma (Γ) is the change in delta (1% spot move for the GEX calculations). Equity and index traders watch gamma exposure (GEX) because it gives clues about how options dealers may have to hedge, and that hedging can influence stock or index price movements. Dealers who sell options become short gamma, as the market rises dealers must buy more and as the market falls, dealers must sell more. This works against them ie. needing to buy in a rising market or sell in a falling market. This can increase volatility and cause breakouts and selloffs to accelerate.

Call Wall

Traders look for the Call Wall, the strike with the largest positive call GEX. This is considered a strong resistance level. Dealers may sell futures as this price is approached.

Put Wall

The Put Wall has the strike with the largest put GEX. Often acts as a support and dealers may buy futures on declines above that level.

Gamma Wall

The Gamma Wall is the strike with the largest net positive gamma exposure: Net GEX = Call GEX + Put GEX (where put GEX is negative). This strike is often where dealer hedging exerts the strongest stabilizing influence.

Zero-Gamma Level

The Zero-Gamma Level is the price where the aggregate market gamma changes sign: Above it is a positive gamma regime, and below it, a negative gamma regime. Many institutional traders consider this the most important gamma metric because it indicates whether dealer hedging is likely to dampen or amplify market moves.

If Spot > Zero Gamma when dealers are long gamma, if the market rises they tend to sell stock/futures to hedge; if the market falls then tend to buy stock/futures to hedge. Their hedging activity tends to oppose the move. Volatility then tends to be dampened, intraday moves tend to be smaller, pullbacks are often bought and rallies often sold.

If Spot < Zero Gramma, dealers are estimated to be short gamma. If the market is up then dealers buy, if market is down dealers sell. Their hedging amplifies the move which can produce larger swings, trendier days, more frequent breakouts and higher realized volatility.

The Total GEX at spot shows whether the regime is positive gamma or negative gama. The amount is the dollar move per 1% spot move according the GEX formula.

Gamma Ladder

Gamma is the change in delta

• Ladder

The ladder shows the impact at the various spot levels.

• Traders that have purchased options

As spot increases the delta increases and the trader needs to sell more of the underlying to stay delta neutral. As spot decrease, the delta decreases and the trader needs to buy more of the underlying. Both instances are done at favorable spot, ie. selling as asset as spot increases and buying an asset as spot decrease.

• Traders that have sold options

The option seller has the delimma of having to buy more of the underlying as spot is increasing, and selling more as the price decreases. The seller's disadvantage is offset by time decay which works in the seller's favour. The ladder helps to visualize the effect in order to put stops where required.
