Binary Options

KPI Binary Options

Cboe KPI binaries settle on a reported company metric, such as quarterly earnings per share, AWS revenue or vehicle production. The strike is in the same units as the metric.

Call pays US$1 when the reported KPI is at or above the strike.

Put pays US$1 when the reported KPI is below the strike.

Each contract pays zero when its condition is unmet.

Eligible U.S. customers can trade Cboe KPI binary options through Robinhood Financial in the app under Predict → Earnings Predictions.

Price a KPI binary

Enter the consensus estimate for the reporting period, if available, or your own forecast. Cboe supplies historical reported values. The calculator takes the expected KPI as an input.

Enter the standard deviation for this release as a percentage of the expected KPI’s magnitude. For an expected KPI of 100, enter 2 for a standard deviation of 2 KPI units. Leave days blank for an undiscounted estimate.

Pricing assumptions

The normal model uses the expected KPI as its mean and allows negative outcomes. The lognormal model requires a positive mean and matches the entered standard deviation. Its call and put probabilities use N(d₂) and N(−d₂), respectively, for positive strikes. Both models multiply the probability by the discounted US$1 payout.

In the normal model, when the expected KPI equals the strike, any positive standard deviation gives a 50% payout probability for each side. With positive uncertainty the models are continuous, so equality carries zero modeled probability even if the company rounds its reported figure. With zero uncertainty, an expected KPI equal to the strike pays the call. Values are model estimates based on your inputs. Market prices can reflect different assumptions and a risk premium.

Reported KPI history

The historical benchmark is the sample standard deviation of year-over-year changes for matching fiscal quarters. It uses log changes, ln(current/prior), when all usable ratios are positive. If any usable ratio is zero or negative, it uses relative changes, current/prior − 1, for the whole series. Quarters with a zero prior value are excluded. This benchmark describes historical changes. The pricing input measures uncertainty around the forecast for the upcoming release.
